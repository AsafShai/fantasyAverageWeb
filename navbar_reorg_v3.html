The domain model — three concentric rings

Every page in the app is about exactly one of these. Ordered by how close it is to you.

the real world, reference data
  • NBA Teams (depth charts)
  • Injuries
Tools disappears. Estimator and Trade are about your league. Projections is about players. "Tool-ness" was never a domain — that's exactly why the drawer rotted into a dumping ground for Trends and Draft Report.
Two real tensions the options below trade against each other: (1) My League holds 6 items — a big drawer is a junk drawer again unless it's subdivided or thinned. (2) League Rankings and Teams are your most-used pages — burying them behind a group is the most expensive click cost in the nav. Pure domain purity costs you exactly there.

Every planned page has an unambiguous home — no judgment call, no re-litigating. That's the payoff of domain over intent. But note all four land in the two drawers that are already the biggest: My League goes to 8, Players to 6-7. Option F's labeled sections are what keeps that readable.

My call: D. Domain grouping everywhere, except League Rankings stays a flat top-level link because it's the page you open most and a domain drawer would tax it every single time. Injuries and NBA stay flat too — a 2-item dropdown spends a click to save one slot in a nav that has room. Group them the day a third NBA page exists. If you want zero exceptions and a rule you never argue with, take E. If you expect the 4 planned pages to land soon, take F now instead of migrating to it later.
