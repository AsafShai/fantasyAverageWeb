They have an "Other", and they don't apologize for it. Twelve items, including Trade Analyzer, Playing Time Trends and the Injury Database — pages that in our vocabulary would be analytics, workspaces and NBA reference. A residual bucket is normal on a mature site, not a design failure.

Five findings

Both put Trade in the misc / tools bucket

Hashtag files Trade Analyzer under Other Tools. Basketball Monster files Trade Analysis under Tools, next to Team Analysis. Two independent sites, same call, neither puts it with league or player pages. That's the strongest external support for where we landed on the page you kept doubting.

Neither groups by subject domain

No "League / Players / NBA" split exists on either site. Hashtag groups by what the page outputs; BBM groups by what you paid for. The domain model we built is not the industry pattern — which is worth knowing, though see finding 4 for why it may still be right here.

We've been agonizing over drawer size for nothing

Hashtag's Other holds 12. BBM's Members holds 17. Our largest is 3. Every "is this drawer too big" round we ran was optimizing a constraint neither real site treats as one.

Their model doesn't transfer, and the reason is countable

Hashtag can group by output type because they have six ranking pages and five schedule pages — real clusters. You have one or two of each type. Grouping your 13 pages by output would produce roughly seven groups of two. Their clusters are by type; yours genuinely are by subject.

They're solving a different problem

Both are public content sites: every page is an SEO landing page for strangers, and the nav doubles as a product catalogue. Yours is a private workspace for twelve people who already know what's in it. Their nav sells; yours navigates.

Where this leaves the plan

Thing you were unsure aboutWhat the competitor scan says
Trade Analyzer placementSettled. Hashtag and BBM both file it in their misc/tools bucket. Keep it in Tools.
Analytics as a group nameFine. Hashtag scatters its analysis pages into "Other" and pays for it — an explicit analytics bucket is better than what they do.
Domain split at allNot the industry pattern, but their alternative needs many pages per type. You have 13 pages total. The output-type split gives you four two-item drawers and a four-item "Other" — strictly worse.
Tools holding one itemBoth sites ship buckets far more lopsided than that. Hashtag's Dynasty group holds 2, and BBM's Monster group is 6 near-duplicate products. A 1-item drawer is not the anomaly you thought.
Drawer sizes generallyStop optimizing. Their biggest are 12 and 17. Yours is 3.
Honest read: the scan didn't turn up a better structure — it turned up two sites solving a different problem, one of them organizing by paywall. What it did do is settle Trade with outside evidence and show that the thing we spent the most rounds on, drawer size, isn't something real sites treat as a constraint.

If anything is still worth stealing, it's Hashtag's honesty about "Other" — the admission that a small residual bucket beats forcing every page into a rule. Our Tools group is exactly that, with the advantage of a written rule and a rejection list.
