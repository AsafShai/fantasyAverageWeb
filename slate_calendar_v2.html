“Which teams are good to pick up”

a query, not advice
The schedule can answer exactly one version of that question honestly: who gives you the most games. In an 8-cat roto league with per-slot game caps, every extra game is raw counting stats against a cap you are trying to fill — so Δ vs league average is the whole signal, and it is a fact, not an opinion. It ranks schedules, never players.

What it deliberately does not do: it does not know who is good, who is injured, who is startable, or who is available in your league. A team at +3 tells you a streamer from that roster gets three more games than average — nothing about whether they should be on your roster. The same posture as the rest of this page.

Column by column

The dot row is the point. It is the only place in the app that answers "when does this team actually play" without opening one team at a time, and it is what makes the strip clickable in both directions: pick a night, see who is on; pick a team, see its nights.

How much signal is actually in Δ — measured, not assumed. On this real window: over 7 days every team plays 3 or 4 games — the column is binary and close to worthless. Over 14 days the range is 5–8, but 26 of 30 teams sit at 7 or 8. Over 28 days it is 12–15 with 25 of 30 at 14–15. So Δ is a tails signal: it says nothing about most of the league and a lot about the four teams at the edges. That is worth showing — the two teams at 5 games over a fortnight are a genuine 60% shortfall — but it is not the continuous ranking it first looks like, which is why it now reads +1 / — / -2 in whole games rather than +0.9. A count difference does not have a tenths place, and printing one made 26 identical teams look separable.

B2B is kept and stays neutral-coloured. A back-to-back is two games in two nights — good for volume, bad for minutes and rest. Whether that is a plus or a minus depends on whether you are chasing counting stats or percentages, so the view states it and stops.

Light nights were cut. They were a colour on the strip in v1. Cut per your call — and the concept went with the colour rather than surviving as a column, since keeping "games on light nights" while removing the light-night marking would have left a column pointing at something the chart no longer shows.
