Picking a team is now a button

fixed
What was wrong. In v2 the whole POR @ NOP chip was one click target, and it guessed which team you meant — clicking cycled POR → NOP → off depending on what was already selected. Two teams, one target, hidden state machine. Nothing about the chip said it was clickable either: no border, no hover affordance, same flat grey as every other label on the page.

What it is now. Each abbreviation is its own <button> with a border, a hover state and a focus ring — it looks like a control because it is one. The @ between them is plain text and not clickable, so the two targets are visually separated rather than sharing a box. Clicking POR selects POR. Clicking it again clears. No guessing.

Same treatment in the grid. The team column was a bare sticky cell you were expected to discover was clickable from a caption. It is the same button component now, so the two places you can pick a team look identical and behave identically.

And the selection is now visible as state. A banner names the traced team and carries an explicit Clear — in v2 the only way out was to remember to click the same row again.

Dynamic windows — yes, with one real limit

today → any date
Worth adding. The fixed 14/28 presets answered "the next fortnight" and nothing else, but the questions people actually have are dated: through the end of the month, through the deadline, through the last day. A date input answers all of them and the presets stay as shortcuts.

The cap is the schedule, not a preference. Max selectable date is the last day the schedule has — calendar_days[last].date, Apr 11 here. That is the same bound ESPN's whitelist calendar gives, because both come from the published schedule; the frontend should read it off the payload rather than hardcode a season end, so it stays correct when the NBA Cup games get filled in and the range grows.

Long windows just scroll — reverted from v3. v3 had the strip thin its bars and drop labels past 21 days, and the grid swap dots for weekly counts past 35. Both are gone. Every window now renders identically: one bar per night at a fixed width, one dot per night, and the block scrolls sideways.

Empty stretches are now drawn. The All-Star break (Feb 19-24 this season) is six consecutive zero-game days. A zero-height bar reads as a rendering bug, so those nights get a dotted baseline and a greyed count. Pick "Rest of season" to see it.

Why the two "smart" modes were removed

v4
They solved a problem that was already solved. Both existed to avoid a horizontal scrollbar — but this table was always going to scroll sideways, and the codebase's own rule for wide tables is scroll with a sticky anchor column, which the grid already had. The team name stays pinned while the nights scroll past it, so 97 columns are no worse to read than 28; there is just more of them.

What they cost. Two extra rendering paths, two extra legends, two threshold constants, and a card that looked like a different feature depending on the window length. A 14-day view and a rest-of-season view should be the same thing at two lengths — if they render differently, you have to re-learn the chart every time you change the range.

The weekly grid also quietly destroyed the point. "Who plays when" is a question about nights. Bucketing to "4 games that week" cannot answer whether a team plays on the Thursday you are trying to fill, which is the only reason to open the grid. It answered a question nobody asked in order to fit a narrower box.

The honest cost of scrolling: at rest-of-season the grid is ~1500px wide, so comparing the first week to the last means scrolling rather than glancing. That is a real loss — and still cheaper than two modes. The strip above stays a fixed-height overview, so the shape of the whole window is never more than one glance away.
Δ gets stronger as the window grows — and that is the argument for the date picker. Over 7 days every team plays 3 or 4 games and the column is noise. Over the rest of the season the spread is wide enough to actually rank. The preset that was hardest to justify in v2 is the one the custom range fixes.
Still frontend-only. Every number here comes from the one cached /api/nba/schedule payload: calendar_days[] for the strip, per-team games[] for the dots, counts, B2Bs and matchups. Changing the window refetches nothing — it re-slices an array already in memory.
