Making NBA pace actually visible

You're right — a 2px dashed line inside a 15px track carries too much meaning for how quiet it is. Same scenario in all three: NBA pace 68.4 GP/team, so a perfectly-used 82-slot would sit at 68.4 today and UTIL at 205.2. C is 16.4 games behind pace, F is 10.4 behind, SG is 2.6 ahead. Whichever variant you pick, those three facts should be obvious without reading a number.

What changed: dashed → solid, taller than the track, plus a downward caret and a one-time "NBA PACE" label on the top row. Cost: needs ~14px of headroom per row for the caret, and when the pace marker and the est. tick land close together (PG here: 83.4% vs 96.3%) two near-identical black lines compete. Reads well, but it is still asking a thin line to do the work.

Why this one. The thing you care about is the gap, not the line — so draw the gap. The hatched band runs from where you are to where pace sits, and its width is the deficit at a glance: C's band is unmissable, F's is clearly smaller, SG has none because it is ahead (shown instead as a lighter blue overrun past the pace boundary).

No new colour. Stripes are neutral ink at 16% — they read as "missing" without introducing a third hue next to blue and red. No extra height: everything lives inside the existing 16px track, so the card stays at its current size.

How it reads: the darker grey runs 0 → pace. Blue sits on top, so a slot behind pace leaves visible grey sticking out past the blue, and a slot ahead has blue overshooting the grey entirely. Trade-off: quietest of the three and needs zero extra height, but "grey I can still see" is a subtler cue than a hatched band, and it collides visually with the light-grey "rest of cap" unless the two greys stay well separated — which constrains the palette in dark mode.

One extra chip, vs pace −16, only on slots that are actually off pace. This is not the old Rate row returning — that printed a delta in all eight cells and forced two lines per cell. Here it is one chip in a row that already exists, and it stays silent when a slot is on pace, so the page only spends ink where something is wrong.
