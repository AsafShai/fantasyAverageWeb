How this is fetched

no per-day requests
Switching 14 → 28 days fetches nothing. The whole season arrives in one shot — 7 calls to scoreboard?dates=YYYYMM, fired together under a single asyncio.gather, giving 1200 games and 156 game days. That result is cached for 24h. Coverage is then a slice of memory: filter the cached games to your roster's proTeamIds over a date window and count. Zero network per horizon change, per team switch, or per page revisit inside the TTL.

Why 24h and not longer. Two things move: postponements, and the Cup fill-in games appearing in December. A daily refresh catches both. Nothing needs to be stored in Postgres — this is a cache, not a dataset, and it rebuilds in one round-trip if the process restarts.

What is not cached: your roster. That comes from kona_player_info on its existing 5-minute cache, so an add/drop shows up in coverage within minutes without touching the schedule cache at all.
Cut: the slot-deficit table. You asked whether it belongs here — it doesn't. It's the same eight numbers as tab ①, re-rendered. Tab ① already shows deficit as the gap between the bar and the cap, and reachability as the red block. Showing it twice makes the page look like it has two features when it has one.

Schedule fetching

your call, adopted
Parallel, not sequential. The seven month calls have no ordering dependency, so they go through scoreboard_async under one asyncio.gather — one round-trip's latency instead of seven. The sync client.scoreboard is the wrong tool here: it carries SLEEP_BETWEEN_CALLS = 0.15 pacing meant for the nightly bulk pull's hundreds of sequential requests, and it would block the event loop.

Cached daily. A published schedule is near-static, so a 24h TTL is right — but not "fetch once and forget": postponements happen, and the Cup fill-in games appear in December. Daily refresh catches both without hammering ESPN. Same cache_manager pattern the other providers use, plus the ETag support data_provider already relies on.

Placement — recommendation

split them
You're right that ④ is an NBA-team picker — and you already have one. NbaTeams.tsx holds selectedTeamId, syncs it to a ?team= search param, and renders DepthChartView beneath it. Tab ④ is a second view under that same picker: depth chart and schedule are two things you want about one NBA team. A new page would mean a second team dropdown and a second URL param for the same concept.

Tab ③ is the opposite shape — all 30 teams, no picker, nothing to select. It wants its own route, /schedule, under the NBA nav group.

So: split. ③ standalone page · ④ a tab inside NbaTeams · ① and ② on TeamDetail. Three homes, each matching what the page is already about. They share one cached schedule service underneath, but shared data is not a reason to share a page.

The one cost: ③ and ④ answer related questions and now sit a click apart. Worth it — a "Schedule" page carrying both a league grid and a team picker would be two features wearing one hat, which is what v1 got wrong.
